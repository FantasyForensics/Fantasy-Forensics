Read it in players, not points

Translated into player equivalents, the ladder stops being abstract. An early 2027 1st (5,916) prices like Malik Nabers (5,949). A mid 1st (4,448) is Jalen Hurts (4,462) money. A late 1st (3,877) sits just over Derrick Henry (3,849). An early 2nd (3,075) is roughly Jaylen Waddle (3,034); an early 3rd (1,983) is Keaton Mitchell (1,980). Two gaps matter most. The early-to-late spread inside the 1st round alone is 2,039 points — more than an early 3rd is worth by itself — which is why “a 2027 1st” is not a price until you know whose 1st it is. And the famous cliff after the 1st round is shallower than folklore says: late 1st (3,877) to early 2nd (3,075) is a 21% step, not a halving. Even the late rounds carry real prices — a mid 3rd (1,867) is worth more than half the players on the board, 207 of 401 — which is why our calculator never treats one as a free throw-in.

Future years discount gently

The year-out discount is shallower than production value alone would set, because the market keeps paying up for future picks. A mid 2028 1st (3,610) still holds 81% of the mid 2027 1st's price; a mid 2029 1st (3,264) holds 90% of the 2028. The practical read cuts against habit: contenders shouldn't treat a 2028 1st as a throw-in, and rebuilders collecting 2027 1sts are holding the most liquid asset in the format.
