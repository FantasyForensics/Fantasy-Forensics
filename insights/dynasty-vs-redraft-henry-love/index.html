Same Points, Opposite Prices: Why Dynasty and Redraft Values Diverge

Through five weeks, Derrick Henry and Jordan Love are producing identical points. Redraft prices Henry 54% higher; dynasty prices Love 39% higher. Both are right — they're answering different questions.

Derrick Henry and Jordan Love are, right now, effectively the same player. Through Week 5 in this league's scoring, Henry averaged 22.4 points per game; Love averaged 22.6. Our rest-of-season projections keep them level the rest of the way — 21.9 vs. 22.2 points per week. And yet the two formats on this site price them in opposite directions, by a lot. That flip is the entire difference between dynasty and redraft, contained in one pair of names.

Redraft asks one question

Redraft asks a single question: who scores more between now and Week 17? By that measure Henry is the prize — a single-season value of 6,767 against Love's 4,392, a 54% edge. He is a workhorse back producing RB1 points today, and in a format where next season doesn't exist, nothing else about him matters. The age curve is somebody else's problem.

Dynasty asks a longer one

Dynasty asks what a player is worth across every season you'll own him, and the answer flips: Love 5,562 (QB11) against Henry 3,994 (RB17) — a 39% edge the other way. Two forces do it. The first is the age curve: Henry is 32, and in our measured curves running back value falls off a cliff in the late 20s — and keeps falling. That is why our algorithm's price on him sits where it does — it discounts his age more steeply than most managers do. The second force is time. Love is 27 at the position that holds value longest — quarterback prices barely move into the early 30s — and in superflex, a stable starting quarterback is the scarcest asset in the format. Love's price contains the better part of a decade of starter seasons. Henry's contains what's left of this one, and maybe the next.

Neither price is wrong; they're answers to different questions — which is why all 401 players on this board carry both.
